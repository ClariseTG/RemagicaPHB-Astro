# Checks and Dice

When a character takes an action with an uncertain outcome, the uncertainty can be resolved with a Check. A Check involves rolling dice, adding modifiers, and comparing the outcome to a Difficulty.

## Dice Notation

Dice are notated as such:

XdY

When a roll is written this way, it means to roll X dice with a maximum value Y. For example, "roll 2d6" means to roll 2 dice that each have 6 sides.

## Advantages and Disadvantages

All sorts of effects, from Skills to Tags, can add Advantages and Disadvantages to a Check. Advantages and Disadvantages stack; if two different effects each add one Advantage, then the Check will have two Advantages, each added to the roll.

If a Check gets an Advantage and a Disadvantage, they cancel each other out.

For example, a Check that has gotten 3 Advantages and 2 Disadvantages would have 1 Advantage in practice.

## Making a Check

When a character makes a Check, they roll 1d20 and add Modifiers. These modifiers are as follows:

- The relevant [Statistic](/characters/statistics.md) is added directly to the roll.

- The relevant [Proficiency](/characters/statistics.md) is also added directly to the roll.

- If the Check has Advantages or Disadvantages, roll Nd6, where N is the number of Advantages or Disadvantages the Check has. If they are Advantages, add this number to the original roll. If they are Disadvantages, subtract this number from the original roll.

The number is then compared to the Difficulty of the Check. This number is usually around 11, but the GM can raise it or lower it depending on how likely it is the character will manage to succeed.

For example, Borro wants to scare someone with her imposing stature, right after patching up a teammate's wound. Her shirt is freshly stained with her teammate's blood. How scary she is, exactly, is unclear.

She could use Body for the Check's Statistic and Intimidation for its Proficiency. Since she's covered in blood, the GM gives her an advantage.

Now, she rolls:

1d20 + Body + Intimidation + Advantage

1d20 + 3 + 1 + 1d6

12 + 3 + 1 + 2

The result of this Check is 18.

A Check is successful if the result is greater than or equal to the Difficulty. However, the difference between the Difficulty and the result can be used to divine the specifics of how the event plays out.

Since 18 is a good bit higher than 11, Borro's victim is certainly scared. If she had rolled even higher-- say, 10 or more above the Difficulty-- the victim would be terrified. If Borro's Check had a result of 11 or 12, which is just barely passing, the victim might not be that *scared*, but uncomfortable enough to let Borro get her way.

The specific readings of dice rolls are very vibe-based. The meaning of a dice roll is always up for discussion between players and the GM, but the GM has the final say on what happens in the end.
